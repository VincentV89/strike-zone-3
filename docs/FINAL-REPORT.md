## B. Implemented Features

- **Maps:** Desert Compound (outdoor compound: buildings and walls, courtyards, narrow corridors, crates and barriers, ramps to elevated positions, four spawn zones) and Industrial Warehouse (warehouse interior with shipping containers, storage racks, walkways and platforms, yard and back lane, five spawn zones). Both are deterministic TypeScript data with procedural materials.
- **Weapons:** assault rifle (automatic, 30 + 180 rounds, 25 body / 63 head damage), pistol (semi-automatic, 15 + 90, 18 / 45, fastest reload), sniper rifle (semi-automatic, 5 + 25, 90 / 180, hold RMB for a 4× scope with reduced spread). Every weapon has magazine and reserve ammo, reload, switching (1/2/3 or wheel), a fire cooldown, recoil and spread. Player starts with 100 health and 100 armor; armor absorbs half of each hit while it lasts.
- **CPU AI:** a seven-state machine per bot (Patrol, Investigate, Engage, Take Cover, Flank, Retreat, Dead) with sight (range, field of view, line of sight) and hearing of player gunfire only, a reaction delay and imperfect aim, A* paths on a baked waypoint graph, soft separation and a stuck watchdog. Senses run at 20 Hz and decisions at 10 Hz, staggered across bots.
- **Supported bot counts:** 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 10, 15 and 20.
- **Difficulty levels:** Easy, Normal and Hard change reaction time, aim error, aggression, cover and flank use and pauses between bursts, never health.
- **Game mode:** single-player elimination round against CPU bots with a round timer (2, 3, 5 or 10 minutes). Outcomes are Victory (all bots eliminated), Defeat (player killed) and Time Expired (a loss).
- **HUD and menus:** health, armor, weapon and ammo, crosshair, remaining enemies, kills, timer, hit and headshot markers, damage direction arcs, kill feed, radar showing only detected bots, hold-Tab scoreboard; main menu with match setup, settings (sensitivity, FOV, master and effects volume, graphics preset, round duration), controls, about and match history; loading, ready gate, countdown, pause, eliminated and end-of-round screens. Synthesised Web Audio sound effects. Settings and history persist in localStorage.

## C. Technical Architecture

- **Game engine / renderer:** three.js 0.187 (WebGLRenderer, WebGL2) behind one `GameRenderer` that only reads simulation snapshots and events. Textures are painted in code; no image, model, sound or font file ships.
- **Frontend framework:** none. TypeScript 6 with plain DOM screens built by Vite 8 into one static bundle.
- **Physics engine:** hand-written TypeScript collision world: upright capsules against boxes and ramp wedges on a 4 m grid, with movement, ray casts, line of sight and separation all reading the same shape list.
- **AI architecture:** bot-ai components for perception, locomotion and a finite state machine brain, scheduled at 20 Hz sense and 10 Hz decide. Perception is the only code that reads the player, and only through sight and hearing; arch tests enforce this information firewall.
- **Navigation:** a waypoint graph baked per map in time-sliced steps (box-top lattice plus ramp and anchor nodes), A* with string pulling, at most 2 searches per tick and a path cache.
- **Simulation:** a deterministic, headless 60 Hz fixed-step game simulation with seeded random streams. It runs unchanged in Node tests and in the browser.
- **AWS services:** one CDK v2 TypeScript app with one stack: a private S3 bucket (Block Public Access, SSL only) behind a CloudFront distribution with Origin Access Control, a response headers policy (CSP, HSTS, frame and content-type options) and SPA error mapping. Two deploy-time Lambda custom resources publish the bundle and empty the bucket on destroy.
- **Reasons for major decisions:** a static bundle needs no servers and costs about $0; a hand-written collision and nav layer keeps the simulation dependency-free and deterministic for headless tests; three.js is permissively licensed with no dependencies; the single information firewall keeps bots fair; seeded randomness makes every gameplay test reproducible.

## Known Limitations

- **KL-01.** Performance evidence is indirect: WebGL on the build runtime is software-rendered and every timing is Node CPU time on 2 vCPU, mostly under v8 coverage. GPU frame time and 1080p FPS are manual (MT-05).
- **KL-02.** On the Industrial Warehouse a bot can stay stuck for more than 5 s (bot-ai harness worst span 540 ticks against 300; it was 720 before the graphics uplift added prop colliders). The cause is a collision-nav defect: sweepClear accepts segments at ramp sides that moveCapsule blocks, and only the watchdog snap frees the bot. No bot stays stuck unresolved.
- **KL-03.** The worst simulation tick at 20 bots exceeds the 6 ms target under coverage (about 10–25 ms; about 3.4 ms without coverage). Means stay well inside their budgets.
- **KL-04.** The live 3D round is not screen-reader playable; menus and overlays are keyboard operable.
- **KL-05.** Saved settings and match history live in localStorage and are tied to the CloudFront origin: cdk destroy plus a fresh deploy, or a custom domain, loses them.
- **KL-06.** The SEC-GS-09 tick-path fuzz was split into 2,000 × 60 ticks at 1 bot plus 20 × 600 ticks at 20 bots, because the full 2,000 × 600 at 20 bots would add 15–50 minutes to verify.
- **KL-07.** Real pointer lock, audible audio, GPU frame time and WebGL context loss can only be checked by a person in a desktop browser (MT-02, MT-03, MT-05, MT-06).
- **KL-08.** Browser end-to-end tests run in Playwright headless Chromium with SwiftShader WebGL and the pointer-lock bypass seam against the dist-test bundle, not against production dist/ and not in Firefox or Safari.
- **KL-09.** Graphics uplift rule changes (disclosed per req-gfx-disclosure): third-party CC0 binary assets ship under assets/ with ProvenanceEntry v2 (Poly Haven, Quaternius; credits in docs/CREDITS.md); the src/render loader ban became an allow-list of GLTFLoader, KTX2Loader, the meshopt decoder and SkeletonUtils in src/render/assets/kit.ts, loaded by a second memoised import() in main.ts; large props collide and block sight and shots, so the seed-42 golden was regenerated; world ceilings rose to 80/100/120 calls and 300k/600k/1M triangles; High shadow casters rose to 32.
- **KL-10.** CSP rule change: script-src adds 'wasm-unsafe-eval' and 'unsafe-eval', and worker-src 'self' blob: is added. 'unsafe-eval' was not in the approved RA-G3 set: three 0.187's Basis transcoder (an Emscripten embind build) calls new Function in its worker, and the requester approved adding it (CG-G-Q1 = A, 2026-11-20). img-src blob: was approved but is not needed and is not added.
- **KL-11.** The near-camera detail layer (BR-G-71) was cut first in the agreed cut order: detailLayer is false on every preset. Near-camera resolution comes from 2048 px viewmodel and bot textures with mip-drop tiers. Rule-generated facade geometry was replaced by PBR facade texture sets plus prop dressing, the 512 px contact AO map by none (dressing receives the sun shadow), and the dressing chunk-cell split and the up-tier refetch were not built.
- **KL-12.** The bot character is a stylised low-poly CC0 soldier (Quaternius) with a rifle skinned to the hand; the source pack has no crouch clips, so crouch-idle and crouch-walk are derived offline from its SitDown pose. Viewmodels are low-poly CC0 Quaternius weapons with a baked colour palette, without normal maps.
- **KL-13.** Compressed equirectangular KTX2 skies do not convert to a background cube in three 0.187, so the PMREM environment (cube UV, 256 px faces) is also the sky background; the sky is softer than the source photo.
- **KL-14.** The entry chunk is 204,218 B brotli against the 204,800 B check:seams limit (582 B headroom); asset code that runs after the kit loads lives in the lazy kit chunk (47,749 B brotli, 174,296 B minified, over the 150 KB reported target and under the 2x ceiling). Screenshot sign-off against the reference image (MT-G1) and the 60 FPS cells on GTX 1660 / RX 580 class hardware (MT-G2, MT-G3) are manual and not recorded yet.
